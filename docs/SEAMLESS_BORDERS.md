# Seamless borders

A converted world ends somewhere. Past that edge, the game generates new terrain with its own
generator and the world's seed. WorldBridge's goal is that no conversion, to any edition or version,
leaves cut chunks or exposed walls where the two meet. When a target has no remedy yet, the
conversion says so.

## Where the seam comes from

There is a step wherever the converted terrain at the edge differs from what the game would
generate there. This happens when:

- the world comes from another edition or version, so another generator made it;
- the world was cut (a chunk selection, a trim) or compressed in height;
- the seed does not match the terrain, as in LCE or PE worlds taken elsewhere.

There is no step only when source and target share **the same generator and the same seed**, for
example Java 26.3 → 26.3 cut down, Beta → Beta, or LCE → LCE of the same title update.

## The three remedies

| Remedy | When | Needs an exact generator? |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Game blending** | Java ≥ 1.18 and Bedrock ≥ 1.18. Chunks written in the pre-1.18 format are blended by the game itself when it upgrades them, and it generates the new depth below y 0. | No |
| **B. Fill the whole world** | Small finite worlds: LCE maps of 54 or 64 chunks, PE 0.x (16 × 16 chunks). If every chunk exists, the game generates nothing, so no seam can appear. | No: natural terrain that meets the converted edge is enough |
| **C. Ring with the game's generator** | Infinite worlds that do not blend (Java Alpha 1.2 – 1.17) and neoLegacy. The ring starts from the converted heights and reaches, at its outer edge, terrain **identical** to what the game will generate. | Yes: reproduced block for block and checked on worlds generated by the game |

The game's blending (A) and WorldBridge's ring and fill (B, C) are two separate options: *Blending
del gioco* / `--no-blend` and *Anello di raccordo* / `--no-ring`.

Every target without a remedy (Bedrock 1.1 – 1.17, Customized worlds, large console maps) warns at
the end of the conversion (`terrain/policy.py`).

**Sea level.** Alpha 1.2 – Beta 1.7.3 and PE 0.x have the sea at y 63, every later game at y 62. The
converted world moves by one block in the right direction, with players and spawn; otherwise a step
of water would remain even where the ground meets.

## How the ring works

The ring does not depend on a particular generator:

1. **Look at the converted border.** Ground height and trees of the converted chunks along the
   edge.
2. **Measure the difference.** At the border, the converted ground minus the generator's ground.
3. **Spread it outwards.** The difference is carried outwards by the smoothest possible surface: a
   harmonic function solved once over the whole ring on 4-block cells, equal to the difference at
   the border and to 0 at the outer edge. It has no seams at chunk borders and no streaks.
4. **Generate the ring chunks, lifted.** Each chunk is generated by the game's generator with its
   **density field raised or lowered** by that amount, in fractions of a block, before the surface
   and the beaches. Hills, valleys and overhangs stay the game's own. Caves and ravines, carved by
   the game in unlifted terrain, move with the rock. A noise bends the lift lines, which would
   otherwise draw rings around the converted world; at the outer edge the lift is always zero.
5. **Add the border's plants.** Trees (counted from crown cover and from trunks near the ground),
   tall grass and flowers of the converted border, thinning out outwards. The game then decorates
   the ring chunks with its own trees, ores and lakes.
6. **Fix the biomes under the sea.** Where the ring goes down under water to reach a converted sea
   floor, a land biome from the generator would make the game decorate the sea as land (lily pads
   and swamp trees in the ocean). Those columns take an ocean biome (frozen ocean where the biome
   is cold).
7. **Seal the water.** The ring never goes below the sea where the converted border is dry (a dry
   canyon ends against a rock wall, like a real canyon head). A final pass turns into ground every
   ring block where water or lava would touch the air of the converted world, or converted water
   the air of the ring. The converted world is never modified.

**Width.** The largest height difference at the border divided by the slope (one block every two
columns), from 3 to 12 chunks. Where the converted ground is higher than the game's, the lift lasts
longer, so land stays next to the border and the game's sea shore comes after it.

**Chunk status.** Ring chunks are written as "not populated" (older targets) or with status
`surface` / `base` / `noise` (1.13+), exactly like chunks the game has just generated, so the game
adds its own decoration.

## The Nether and the End

**Generators.** They are read from the jars and checked with a harness that loads the jar and calls
the game's generator with the same seed:

- **Nether, Alpha 1.2 – 1.12** (`terrain/nether.py`):
  - a 5 × 17 × 5 density field (two 16-octave noises blended by an 8-octave one, minus a cosine
    curve that closes the floor and the roof): netherrack where positive, lava under y 32;
  - bedrock 0–4 blocks thick at the top and the bottom; soul sand and gravel between y 60 and 65;
  - caves in three forms: Beta, Beta 1.8 – 1.4.7, and 1.5 – 1.12. The Beta-form Nether caves are
    rebuilt from the Beta 1.7.3 cave code already verified for the Overworld; without a Beta jar
    they could not be compared with the game (density and surface are the same as b1.8.1, and
    those are verified).
- **Nether 1.13**: the same density; the surface comes from the surface builder, and the bedrock
  is written afterwards on a 17 × 17 area that wraps around the chunk, as the game does.
- **End 1.0 – 1.8**: the main island (100 − 8 × the distance in cells). **1.9 – 1.12** add the
  outer islands (simplex noise) beyond 1,024 blocks. **1.13**: the island noise has its own seed,
  and the surface builder removes the top block where its noise is low; End biomes are computed.
- **Nether and End 1.14 – 1.17** (`terrain/dims16.py`): the NoiseChunkGenerator with the "nether"
  and "end" settings. Here the ring is written at the "noise" stage: biomes (soul sand valleys,
  basalt deltas, forests), surfaces, caves and decoration come from the game.

**3D ring** (`terrain/ring3d.py`). The Nether is a world of caverns and the End of floating islands,
so there is no height field to lift. Instead, each ring column blends two rock / void sequences:

- the one of the nearest converted border column, looked up from a point moved by a noise, so rock
  does not stretch into straight prisms;
- the generator's own.

Both become signed distances (blocks to the nearest rock / void change along x, z or y) and are
mixed with a weight of 1 at the converted border and 0 at 4 chunks. The lines of equal weight are
bent by a noise, so a game island entering the ring is not cut straight. Floors, roofs and islands
slide from one height to the other; lava stays under y 32 and the bedrock is the generator's.
Buildings, fortresses, glowstone and pillars at the border are not stretched (they are not terrain).

## neoLegacy's world generator

Read from neoLegacy's source (`Minecraft.World`) and compared with a world generated by the game:

- **Biomes.** `Layer::getDefaultLayers` resembles Java 1.7 – 1.8, but 4J changed it:
  - the warm climate draws from the 1.1 list (desert, forest, hills, swamp, plains, taiga; no
    savanna);
  - biomes are not zoomed before edges and hills (Java: two zooms), so they are four times larger
    while rivers stay the same;
  - the hills noise is only 2 or 3, so every hill spot becomes a hill or an "M" biome;
  - mushroom islands, shores and rivers follow 4J's rules.

  No Java version gives the same biomes (22 % at most).
- **Terrain.** The density of Java 1.1 – 1.6 (128 high), with biome depth and scale from
  neoLegacy's `Biome.cpp`. In the last 32 blocks before the map edge the ground sinks into the sea,
  and the last column is sea over stone.
- **Surface.** `Biome::buildSurfaceAtDefault` and its variants: bedrock at y 0–2, gravel and stone
  in the hills, podzol in the giant taiga, puddles in swamps, clay bands and pillars in the mesa.
- **Caves and ravines.** Those of TU19 (the same as Java 1.0 – 1.1), with one difference due to the
  compiler: MSVC evaluates function arguments right to left, so when a tunnel branches the game
  draws the width before the branch's seed.
- **Saves.** New neoLegacy worlds carry a `region_format_16` marker: regions of 16 × 16 chunks.

## How a generator is accepted

Every generator is accepted only after tests against the real game:

1. **The version's jar.** The code of `ChunkProviderGenerate`, `MapGenCaves`, `MapGenRavine`,
   `BiomeGenBase` and similar classes is read (decompiled, for reference only, never included in
   WorldBridge).
2. **A reference world generated by the game.** A new world with a fixed seed and a few hundred
   chunks explored.
3. **Automatic comparison.** Biomes identical to cubiomes; on undecorated chunks an identical
   surface; overall ≥ 97 % of the columns. The differences must all fall in the game's decoration.
4. **A test in the repository** with some columns of the reference world, so that a later change
   cannot break it silently.
5. **An in-game check** of the ring's outer edge: fly past it and see no step.

The figures for every generator are in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md).

## Code layout

```
worldbridge/terrain/
  policy.py      chooses the remedy for a conversion: game blending, ring, fill, a warning, or off
  ring.py        the ring (any generator): lift, width, trees, sea biomes, water sealing
  lift.py        lifting a generator's density field
  trees.py       trees, grass and flowers of the converted border, in the old games' shapes
  ring3d.py      the 3D ring around a Nether or End
  filler.py      fast natural terrain that fills finite worlds (remedy B)
  betagen.py     Alpha 1.2 – Beta 1.7.3
  releasegen.py  Beta 1.8 – 1.6
  carvers.py     caves and ravines of Beta 1.8 – 1.12 (and 4J's variant)
  modern.py      1.7 – 1.12
  modern16.py    1.13 – 1.17 (caves and decoration left to the game)
  genlayers.py   biomes b1.8 – 1.6 (port of cubiomes)
  layers17.py    biomes 1.7 – 1.17 (port of cubiomes, 1.13+ oceans and the 1.15+ 3D zoom included)
  neogen.py      neoLegacy terrain
  neolayers.py   neoLegacy biomes
  nether.py      Nether, Alpha 1.2 – 1.13
  end.py         End, 1.0 – 1.13
  dims16.py      Nether and End, 1.14 – 1.17
```

The policy writes its choice in the conversion log: "game blending", "ring of N chunks", "world
filled", or "no border for this version: use X". No conversion leaves an exposed border without
saying so.

## Limits

- **Exactness.** One wrong constant is enough for the outer edge not to match, which is why every
  generator goes through the comparison with the game.
- **Caves at the edge.** Even with an exact ring, the game's caves and decoration near the outer
  edge are created later, in the game's chunks. Underground this looks as it does in the game
  itself.
- **Trees and buildings cut by a selection** stay cut: the game generates new terrain next to them
  but does not rebuild them. Detached leaves decay by themselves.
- **Still missing:** Infdev and Alpha 1.0 – 1.1 (their jars and a reference world are needed),
  Bedrock 1.1 – 1.17, and the generators of the large console maps.
